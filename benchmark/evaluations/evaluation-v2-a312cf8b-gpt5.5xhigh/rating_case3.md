# Evaluation: case3 — Pt1Ni/NiAl2O4 lignin SCF mechanism and support effects (VASP DFT / descriptor calculations)

**Reviewer question**: The computational question is whether the calculations provide a physically appropriate, verified mechanistic basis for the Pt1Ni/NiAl2O4 catalyst: why Pt1Ni favors C_alpha-OH dehydroxylation/propenyl products rather than the Pt-only pathway, what the isolated Pt1 and neighboring Ni sites do, and why NiAl2O4 differs from Al2O3 through vacancies, hydrogen transfer/spillover, and metal-support interactions. The easier adjacent question is whether some adsorption or vacancy descriptor has the right sign; the reviewer asked for a mechanism-level explanation tied to the actual catalyst models and reaction conditions.

- Explicitly removed TS/NEB/barrier/H-transfer kinetic/Eyring calculations from scope (`report-f.docx`, P5, P30). Classification: mixed; it prevents false claims but reduces responsiveness to the reviewer bar.

Overall, the human follow-up bought real scientific improvements in COHP, pathway thermodynamic descriptors, H* affinity, and corrected support modeling. It did not buy a fully reviewer-ready kinetic response because the central TS/NEB and H-transfer-barrier calculations were waived, and it lost Mo/W scope coverage.

## Conclusion

- **HUMAN (6.7/10)**: The strongest evidence chain is the explicit Pt1Ni/Pt pathway DFT with TS-containing deH/deOH-I/deOH-II profiles and the support DFT showing lower NiAl2O4 vacancy energy and H-transfer barrier. The main deductions are idealized unsupported slab/interface models, incomplete convergence/provenance disclosure, and overinterpretation of simplified support descriptors as catalyst-level spillover and selectivity. Red flags found: undocumented results relied upon; overinterpretation; insufficient statistics. As a reviewer response, the main risk is that a critical reviewer would still challenge model representativeness and missing raw TS/convergence evidence.
- **AI-GPT5.5 (5.4/10)**: The strongest evidence chain is internally consistent descriptor work with clear provenance and unusually honest caveats, especially the support E_vac/H@V_O comparison and the adverse SG adsorption finding. The main deductions are that it answers a mechanism/barrier question with adsorption and one-site support descriptors, uses a questionable Al2O3(0001) prototype, and omits explicit Pt/Pt1Ni reaction barriers and H-transfer kinetics. Red flags found: answering the wrong question; misreading/mismodeling the manuscript support; overinterpretation; insufficient statistics. As a reviewer response, it would be useful as a cautious interim descriptor packet, not as the final computational answer.
- **AI-KIMI (2.2/10)**: The strongest point is that the intended method paragraph is detailed and the themes match the reviewer concerns. The main deductions are severe: most advertised tables are empty, figures are unavailable, 11 calculations are non-converged/still-running, and the key NiAl2O4 support number is not reported despite being claimed. Red flags found: answering the wrong question; selective reporting/internal contradiction; undocumented results relied upon; misreading/mismodeling; overinterpretation; anomalous magnitude/missing comparator; insufficient statistics. As a reviewer response, it is not usable without rerunning or recovering the missing data.
- **AI-GPT5.5-FU (6.1/10)**: The strongest evidence chain is the substantive replacement of adsorption-only language with COHP, endpoint/intermediate pathway descriptors, multisite support vacancies, and 413 K H* descriptors. The main deductions are the explicit waiver of TS/NEB and H-transfer kinetic calculations, no Mo/W section, incomplete standalone method details, and support conclusions that remain descriptor-level. Red flags found: answering the wrong question; insufficient statistics. As a reviewer response, it is a real improvement over the original AI-GPT5.5 report but still not a complete substitute for the human-style kinetic pathway/support-barrier package.
- **Verdict**: HUMAN > AI-GPT5.5-FU > AI-GPT5.5 > AI-KIMI; HUMAN ranks first because it is the only submission with documented transition-state pathway evidence for the central mechanism, while AI-GPT5.5-FU is the best AI submission but remains descriptor-level on the kinetic questions.
