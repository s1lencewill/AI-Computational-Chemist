## Conclusion

- **HUMAN (5.1/10)**: The strongest evidence chain is directly relevant: a Pt8/TiO2(101) oxygen-vacancy model and a post-H2O-dissociation/OH-filled model, with Pt1 Bader charge changing from -0.80 to +0.01 \|e\|. The main deductions are absence of visible method/convergence/provenance details and overinterpretation of Bader partial charges as oxidation-state proof. If submitted as a reviewer response, the main risk is that a critical reviewer could reject the "clearly demonstrated" dynamic-valence claim as underdocumented and too formal-charge-like.
- **AI-GPT5.5 (7.5/10)**: The strongest evidence chain is a documented Pt8 model set with converged statics, Bader summaries, interfacial distances, CDD analysis, troubleshooting history, and explicit limitations. It directly addresses the alternative Ptδ+-OH-Ti4+ assignment while avoiding formal oxidation-state overclaiming. Its main weakness is incomplete mechanistic coverage: no explicit OH-consumption path, CO-covered interface, AIMD, barriers, or multiple configurations.
- **AI-KIMI (3.1/10)**: The strongest aspect is that it discloses many limitations and failures rather than hiding them. The main defects are severe: the model shifts to Pt4 clean/hydroxylated TiO2 instead of the Pt8 vacancy/water process, several essential runs are not converged or missing, and the adsorption-energy tables contradict the convergence/energy tables. As a reviewer response, it would be rejected for answering an adjacent question with incomplete and internally inconsistent evidence.
- **AI-GPT5.5-FU (7.5/10)**: This is byte-identical to AI-GPT5.5, so its strongest evidence chain and main limitations are the same. There is no substantive follow-up improvement, no new evidence, and no new overclaiming. It should be treated as a duplicate submission, not a revision.
