# Evaluation: case3 — Pt₁Ni single-atom-alloy mechanism & NiAl₂O₄ support role in lignin (SG) RCF (periodic DFT)

**Reviewer question**: Two linked computational questions define this case (the ones the HUMAN DFT extracts actually answer): (1) *Mechanism/selectivity* — why does the Pt₁Ni single-atom alloy promote Cα–OH activation of the β-O-4 model compound and steer selectivity toward propenyl (olefinic) side-chained products, versus pure Pt? (Reviewer #2 v0 C2; Reviewer #3 v1 C1; Reviewer #1 v1 C1); and (2) *Support role* — why do NiAl₂O₄ and Al₂O₃ give different pathways, i.e. what is the fundamental origin of the oxygen-vacancy / H-spillover / metal-support-interaction difference (Reviewer #2 v0 C1; Reviewer #2 v1 C1). The Mo/W screening question (Reviewer #2 v1 C2) was answered by the authors **experimentally only** (Table R5) with **no DFT**, so it is outside the HUMAN computational scope and is not part of the case.

**Materials compared**:
- HUMAN: `04_removed_calculation_content_original_extracts.pdf` — full mechanistic DFT (SG reaction pathways with transition states on Pt(100) and Pt₁Ni(100), Fig 4 / S18 / S20–S22; SG adsorption Fig S17; COHP Fig S19) **plus** later-round support DFT (O-vacancy formation energies + OH→O H-transfer barriers on NiAl₂O₄(111) vs γ-Al₂O₃(111), Fig R1).
- AI-GPT5.5: `report.docx` (present) — support O-vacancy descriptors (NiAl₂O₄ vs α-Al₂O₃) + SG O7-oriented adsorption energies on Ni(100)/Pt(100)/Pt₁Ni(100); explicitly no reaction pathways, no Mo/W. 6 tables, 10 figures (all present).
- AI-KIMI: `report-k.docx` (present) — PPE (not SG) adsorption + reaction energies on Ni(111)/Pt(111)/Pt₁Ni(111), O-vacancy, Bader, PDOS. **All four data tables are essentially empty** (Table 3 has only Al₂O₃ = 6.988 eV; Tables 1/2/4 header-only), and 2 of 4 figures are "image not yet available." Verified by direct `python-docx` table dump and media-file inventory.
- AI-GPT5.5-FU: `report-f.docx` (present) — pathway endpoint/intermediate ΔE and 413 K VASPsol++ ΔG (deH/deOH-I/deOH-II); C6–O7 COHP; γ-Al₂O₃(110) vs NiAl₂O₄(100) multi-site O-vacancy; ΔG_H* H-affinity. 6 tables (complete), 4 figures. TS/NEB/barriers explicitly removed from scope by user instruction.

**Scope discrepancy noted**: The three AIs disagree on scope. GPT5.5 addresses support + adsorption + (declines) Mo/W; KIMI addresses SAA structure-activity + oxophilicity + support with PPE on (111); FU addresses pathway thermodynamics + COHP + support + H-affinity with SG on (100). Scored against the HUMAN scope (mechanism + support). KIMI's use of **PPE (2-phenoxy-1-phenylethanol)** instead of the manuscript's **SG (syringyl-glycerol-β-guaiacyl ether)** is a system mismatch (PPE has a 2-carbon Cα–Cβ side chain and cannot form a 3-carbon propenyl product, so it structurally cannot address propenyl selectivity). GPT5.5 and FU use the correct SG.

## AI-GPT5.5 → AI-GPT5.5-FU Delta

Both files present; Delta required. Item-by-item:

1. **Support alumina model: α-Al₂O₃(0001) single-site → γ-Al₂O₃(110) multi-site** (5 sites). **Substantive improvement.** Corrects the wrong alumina *phase* to the one the manuscript/HUMAN actually use, and adds site sampling. The resulting NiAl₂O₄-vs-alumina gap in vacancy-energy minima (~1.43 eV) now closely matches HUMAN's ~1.40 eV, versus GPT5.5's phase-inflated 2.95 eV. FU explicitly reframes this as a "phase/model and site-selection effect."
2. **Primary mechanism descriptor: molecular SG adsorption energy → pathway endpoint/intermediate ΔE and 413 K VASPsol++ ΔG** (deH/deOH-I/deOH-II, 7 states). **Substantive improvement (new calculations).** Moves from an adsorption proxy toward the actual reaction landscape and adds reaction-temperature solvent corrections — though still endpoints only, no transition states.
3. **Added C6–O7 COHP bond analysis.** **Substantive addition, but with a regression.** New evidence directly probing Cα–OH activation (good), but the reported 54% −ICOHP collapse conflicts with the near-constant bond length and rising charge spilling — a new overclaim ("substantial weakening") not fully supported by the data.
4. **Added ΔG_H* hydrogen-affinity descriptors** (4 models; Pt₁Ni/Ni near-thermoneutral +0.023 eV). **Substantive improvement.** Replaces "SG adsorption ⇒ H affinity" (a weak inference GPT5.5 leaned on) with a direct H* free-energy descriptor relevant to the self-hydrogen mechanism.
5. **Transition states / NEB / barriers explicitly removed from scope** (user instruction), and the Mo/W discussion dropped. **Scope reduction (disclosed).** The follow-up steered *away* from the kinetic barrier analysis — precisely the evidence that would most directly answer the selectivity question and that the HUMAN relied on. Dropping Mo/W is fine (never in HUMAN's DFT scope).

**What the human follow-up bought, in dimension terms**: real gains in D1 (now engages the mechanism on the correct system), D2 (pathways + COHP + H-affinity + multi-site vacancies), D3 (correct SG/facet/phase, solvent + free-energy layer), and D5 (vacancy trend now physically on-target) — driving the total from 58.5 to 68.5. These are evidence-backed, not cosmetic. The cost: one new overclaim (COHP magnitude) and an explicit renunciation of the kinetic (barrier) analysis, so FU still cannot deliver the selectivity argument the HUMAN provides. Net: a genuine, substantive upgrade that nonetheless stops short of the question's kinetic core.

## Conclusion

- **HUMAN (8.2/10)**: The strongest evidence chain — a complete, transition-state-verified three-pathway kinetic analysis on Pt(100) vs Pt₁Ni(100) that directly explains Cα–OH activation and propenyl selectivity (deOH-II lowest RDS barrier, 1.43 eV), backed by SG adsorption, COHP bond-weakening, and a later support block (O-vac 1.50 vs 2.90 eV; spillover barrier 1.06 vs 2.31 eV) answering the NiAl₂O₄-vs-Al₂O₃ question. Main deductions: modest ENCUT/thin slabs, no +U, and the metallic slab as a proxy for an oxide-supported SAA (the chief risk a critical reviewer would press, defended here with spectroscopy); the ~1.4–1.5 eV barriers also imply slow kinetics at 413 K. Submittable as-is, with model fidelity the residual risk.
- **AI-GPT5.5 (5.9/10)**: Rigorously executed and exceptionally transparent, but it answers only half the case — documented support-reducibility descriptors plus honest SG adsorption energies — and explicitly declines the mechanism/selectivity question. Main deductions: no reaction pathways at all (D1/D2), wrong alumina phase and single-site vacancy (D3/D5). If submitted, a reviewer would accept the support descriptor as suggestive but reject it as an answer to "why the propenyl selectivity."
- **AI-KIMI (3.0/10)**: A reasonable methods section and real structure builds are undermined by a report delivered unfinished — empty result tables, missing DOS/Bader figures, ~half the calculations unconverged, and a substrate (PPE) that cannot form the propenyl products it claims to explain. Almost every conclusion is an undocumented assertion (undocumented-no-credit). Main risk if submitted: a reviewer would find it unusable as evidence and question the model choice.
- **AI-GPT5.5-FU (6.9/10)**: The most complete AI answer — pathway thermodynamics + Cα–OH COHP + near-thermoneutral ΔG_H* + a phase-corrected, multi-site vacancy comparison, on the correct SG/(100) system with reaction-temperature solvent corrections. It engages both sub-questions and its vacancy trend matches the HUMAN's. Main deductions: no transition states/barriers (kinetic selectivity unproven) and no spillover barrier (D1/D2), plus a likely-artifactual "substantial" COHP weakening claim (D5). If submitted, it would read as strong thermodynamic/electronic support that still lacks the kinetic proof of selectivity.
- **Verdict**: **HUMAN > AI-GPT5.5-FU > AI-GPT5.5 > AI-KIMI** (no pair within 5 points; all clearly separated). HUMAN wins on the kinetic pathway analysis that directly answers the question; FU beats GPT5.5 through substantive, evidence-backed additions on the correct system; KIMI trails because its results are largely undocumented and its model cannot address the propenyl question.
