# Evaluation: case5 — DFT support for the h-RuOx vs anh-RuO2 stability difference in acidic OER / PEMWE (periodic VASP DFT + AIMD)

**Reviewer question**: The reviewer asked the authors to add "more detailed theoretical analysis or computational studies" to *support the observed stability difference* between hydrous/amorphous h-RuOx (less stable, more active) and anhydrous crystalline anh-RuO2 (more stable) in acidic OER — i.e., to computationally back the proposed degradation mechanism (water coordination / proton participation / labile lattice oxygen making h-RuOx unstable). The second-round follow-up drilled into four specifics: (a) how is the h-RuOx model constructed? (b) which anh-RuO2 surface? (c) where is the proton responsible for poor stability? (d) what is the free-energy profile of the four elementary OER steps? (`04` Peer-review p1 "Comment 8"; p2 second-round "Comment 2"; the underlying mechanism is Reviewer #1's Points 1/4/5 in `03` p1, p6).

## Conclusion

- **HUMAN (6.4/10)**: The strongest evidence chain is the direct two-material contrast on the correct *stability* descriptor — lattice-O-vacancy formation 0.447 eV (h-RuOx) vs 2.930 eV (anh-RuO2) — supported by physically appropriate model construction (AIMD amorphous phase, (110) crystalline slab) and OH*/ESP corroboration. Main deductions: a single configuration per material for an amorphous phase (no sampling/error bars), undisclosed reference states, thin per-run documentation, and deferral of the explicitly-requested free-energy profile. As a reviewer response its risk is exactly what a critical referee would press on — that a clean-looking 0.4-vs-2.9 eV difference rests on one snapshot with an undefined reference.
- **AI-GPT5.5 (6.4/10)**: The strongest feature is rigor and honesty — a fully documented, converged VASP workflow with reference formulas, and the discipline to report that surface protonation (−1.072 eV) beats internal uptake (−0.142 eV) rather than force the bulk-intercalation story. Main deduction: it never models the two materials and explicitly cannot establish the stability difference, so despite excellent execution it answers an adjacent, easier feasibility question (red flag 1). Its internal-H value independently corroborates KIMI's proton-insertion number, which is a point in its favor for correctness.
- **AI-KIMI (5.8/10)**: The strongest feature is that it frames the problem as a genuine two-phase comparison (anh-RuO2 vs hydrous surrogate) and directly targets the mechanism — lower Ru valence and a larger proton-insertion driving force (−0.16 vs −0.73 eV). Main deductions: the hydrous phase is a crude crystalline single-defect surrogate in bulk cells (not a surface, not amorphous), the statics are under-converged, the −16 eV dehydration energy is anomalous/unbalanced (red flag 6), and Bader charges are over-read as oxidation states (red flag 5). Usable mechanism argument, but the two correctness problems are what a referee would reject.
- **AI-GPT5.5-FU (6.6/10)**: The strongest feature is that it uniquely delivers *both* a proper AIMD amorphous h-RuOx model and the reviewer's explicitly-requested four-step OER free-energy profile, while being the most transparent submission (refusing to fabricate the ESP/OH quantities it lacked). Main deduction: the delivered free-energy profile is single-material and yields an unphysical 1.89 V overpotential that is left unflagged, so the core stability *difference* still is not demonstrated. Its risk as a reviewer response is that the one genuinely new headline number would itself invite a fresh objection.
- **Verdict**: **AI-GPT5.5-FU ≈ AI-GPT5.5 ≈ HUMAN > AI-KIMI** (top three within 2.0 points → a three-way effective tie: HUMAN wins responsiveness, the GPT5.5 pair wins execution/transparency, and they offset; KIMI trails by ~6–8 points on model soundness and two correctness red flags). No pair among the top three is separated by more than the 5-point tie threshold.
